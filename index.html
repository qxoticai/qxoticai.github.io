    01 · The problem

    Enterprise software runs on the JVM.
    So why does AI run elsewhere?

    Payments, insurance, airlines and public services run on the JVM, while almost all AI tooling assumes Python. So Java teams end up operating a second stack or sending their data to a cloud API, and pay for it in extra infrastructure, per-token bills and data that leaves the building. For many tasks, a smaller model running inside the application is enough. We make that possible.

    05 · The stack

    Every component, written by us.

    Most teams wrap someone else's engine. We wrote ours from the file format up, in pure Java, and maintain the largest Vector API codebase in the world, outside the JDK itself. Every component works standalone: use the whole stack, or just the parts you need.

    1. 01your appPlugs into what you already run.Spring AI · LangChain4j · GraalVM
    2. 02jinferThe inference engine. Runs AI models within the JVM, at native speed.chat · vision · embeddings · speech-to-text · text-to-speech · decision models
    3. 03jotasoonOne tensor API, every chip. Write once, accelerate everywhere.Java · C · CUDA · HIP · Metal · OpenCL · Mojo
    4. 04jamJust a matmul. State-of-the-art quantized matrix multiplication: hand-tuned kernels for several instruction sets, with a pure-Java fallback on the Vector API.SSE3 · AVX2 · VNNI · DOTPROD · Metal
    5. 05toknrollFast, token-perfect tokenizers. Matches the Hugging Face and llama.cpp reference tokenizers token for token, verified by parity tests, while staying competitive with native implementations. Pure Java, zero dependencies.tiktoken · Hugging Face · llama.cpp
    6. 06ggufGGUF reader and writer. llama.cpp's model format, in pure Java, with zero dependencies.GGUF · quantized models
    7. 07safetensorsSafetensors reader and writer. Hugging Face's model format, in pure Java, with zero dependencies.Safetensors · Hugging Face

    With gratitude to llama.cpp, Hugging Face and the entire open-model community. We build on your work.

    07 · Questions

    Asked often.

    Something we didn't cover? Ask us directly.

    Is jinfer free?

    Yes. The inference engine is free and open source under Apache 2.0, and always will be. It is published on Maven Central, and the source code is available on GitHub.

    So what do you sell?

    Expertise. We embed AI into your systems, on the JVM and beyond, and fit it to your task: the smallest model that solves it, fine-tuned and evaluated, fast on your hardware, and shipped however you need, down to a single native binary. The open-source stack is our toolbox; what you get is built around your problem.

    Which models does it run?

    More than 19 architectures, including Gemma, Qwen, GPT-OSS, Llama, Ministral, Granite, Nemotron, LFM, Mellum, SmolLM, MiniCPM, Bonsai, Ling and Laguna, plus Parakeet for speech recognition, Kokoro and Inflect for text-to-speech, and embedding and reranking models. Open-weight, private and fine-tuned models all work as GGUF files, straight from Hugging Face or from your own storage.

    Who do you work with?

    Companies of every size, from startups to enterprises, whose software runs on the JVM in Java, Kotlin, Scala or Clojure, and who want AI without a second stack or sending data out. Many work in finance, insurance, the public sector, industry or software.

    Does it need a GPU?

    No. jinfer runs well on ordinary CPUs, and every benchmark on this page is CPU-only. Support for GPUs and other accelerators is growing through jota, the tensor engine.

    How is this different from a cloud AI API?

    This is embedded AI: it runs as a library inside your application. There is no per-token bill and no extra service to run, and prompts and documents never leave your process. The trade-off is model size: cloud APIs run the largest models on dedicated GPUs, while embedded AI runs smaller models on the hardware you already have. For many tasks, such as classification, extraction, search and transcription, that is enough.

    What makes this different from other local AI?

    Most local AI still runs as a separate server, such as Ollama or a llama.cpp server, that your application calls over the network. jinfer runs inside your application, and that pays off four ways:

    • Faster: on CPU it beats those native engines at prompt processing, with no network round trip.
    • Simpler: no server to operate, just one more dependency, shipped and monitored with the rest of your Java code.
    • Safer: no open port and no data on the wire.
    • Easier: a few lines of plain Java, or the Spring AI and LangChain4j APIs you already know.

    And since we built every layer ourselves, new models, new hardware and performance problems are fixed at the source.

    Does it work with Spring AI and LangChain4j?

    Yes. jinfer plugs into both as a drop-in model provider, and the LangChain4j integration passes LangChain4j's own test kits. Existing code switches to an embedded model with little more than a configuration change. Anything that speaks the OpenAI API can also use jinfer server.
